At the edge of a quiet valley stood a little workshop with a blue door. Each morning, Mara opened the shutters and listened to the river moving over smooth stones. She repaired clocks, radios, and once, a wooden bird whose wings had come loose in a storm.

One rainy afternoon, a traveler arrived carrying a brass compass. Its needle spun in circles, though the traveler said it had always pointed toward home. Mara cleaned the glass, tightened a tiny screw, and set the compass beside the window. The needle slowed, trembled, and turned toward the hills.

The traveler smiled. Beyond those hills, a lamp was glowing in a familiar window. Before nightfall, the road was dry again, the workshop was warm, and the little compass rested quietly in its owner's pocket.

After that, people brought Mara all kinds of broken things. She never promised to make them perfect. She only promised to look closely, work patiently, and see what might be made useful again.
